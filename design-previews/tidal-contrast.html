Color & layout specification

Tidal Contrast

The same teal identity, with a much stronger point of view. The revised patient queue adds a midnight-blue header, slate-blue rows, icy blue actions, and monochrome priority labels. Deep petrol anchors the navigation, a saturated ocean panel gives the dashboard a focal point, and bright glacial aqua adds a refreshing highlight. A mineral-gray canvas separates the dark overview and patient queue.

Why this feels less basic

The composition now has three distinct layers: dark navigation, a colored overview, and a light clinical workspace. The petrol sidebar gives the brand weight. A decorative pulse illustration, dotted grid, and fine circular geometry add character without promotional copy. The illustration is ornamental and does not represent patient measurements. Aqua is concentrated in the brand mark, selected navigation, and overview action, so it feels vivid rather than washed out.

How it stays professional

Patient information sits on dark blue surfaces with pale, legible text. The overview carries the decorative styling; the queue stays consistent and easy to scan. All priority labels share the same neutral treatment.

Queue refresh

The dark blue queue below the overview replaces the white patient cards. Patient identity, detected change, and the review action occupy consistent columns. Priority is expressed with words instead of red, amber, or green. This keeps the visual language calm and technical while preserving the existing order.

The surrounding page palette

Deep petrol#103E46The sidebar and darkest brand anchor. Rich enough to feel substantial without the severity of black.
Ocean panel#115965The main overview background. A saturated teal-blue field that creates a clear focal point.
Glacial aqua#83E3DCLogo tile, active navigation text, pulse illustration, and overview link. Fresh and luminous against petrol; never used as small text on white.
Mineral canvas#E6EEEDThe workspace background. Noticeably deeper than the original mist, helping white cards stand out.
Patient surface#FFFFFFDesign specification panels only. The patient queue uses dark blue surfaces instead of white.
Primary action#086D78Review patient buttons with white text. Darker than the aqua highlight so actions remain crisp on light surfaces.
Action hover#05545DThe deeper teal intended for future button hover states.
Pale sea glass#D4ECE9Initials and low-emphasis accents. A supporting tint rather than the dominant page color.
Main text#14383EPatient names and page headings. A blue-green charcoal that connects the light workspace to the sidebar.
Secondary text#48656APatient details, timestamps, and descriptions. Dark enough to remain readable on the mineral canvas.
Dividers#BCD1D0More visible card outlines and structural separators than the original preview.
Dark-surface text#C1DAD9Supporting text on petrol and ocean surfaces. Softer than white, with enough contrast for readable labels.

Balance: strong framing, quiet patient information

Use approximately 60% light neutral surfaces, 30% dark petrol and ocean, and 10% brand highlights across the desktop composition. This is a design guide, not a strict pixel formula. Avoid adding aqua outlines to every card or turning the entire screen dark: the contrast between the framing and the patient queue is what gives this direction its character.

A clinical queue with a technology aesthetic

The queue uses midnight navy #102C40 for its header, deep blue #193B50 for patient rows, and slate blue #20465C for alternating depth. Ice blue #B7E3F2 makes review actions stand out, while pale text #EFF7FB and muted blue-gray #B4CBD8 keep the information readable. Fine blue borders, small uppercase column labels, and monospace patient identifiers add precision without adding charts that could be mistaken for patient data.

High, medium, and low priorities now use the same neutral pill treatment. Their written labels and the existing highest-priority-first order remain intact. Color no longer represents priority in this queue.
