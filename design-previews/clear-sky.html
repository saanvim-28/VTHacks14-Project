Option 02 / Bright & familiar

Clear Sky

Open, precise, and reassuringly familiar. A medium-deep cornflower blue anchors a cool porcelain canvas. White cards and pale powder-blue accents create a bright, open composition, while navy text supplies structure. This is the most conventional clinical direction of the three, but the generous spacing and soft surfaces keep it from feeling like an older enterprise system.

How the palette should be used

Use blue for the primary action and navigation selection, with powder blue behind the summary and initials. Use navy for headings rather than pure black. Let the white cards occupy most of the viewport; blue should guide the eye to actions rather than frame every piece of information.

Where this direction fits

Best if you want immediate familiarity and a clean, established healthcare identity.

This direction is less distinctive than teal or iris. The visual personality comes from typography and spacing rather than additional colors.

The exact palette

Canvas#F4F7FCThe full workspace background; soft enough to make white cards stand out.
Card surface#FFFFFFPatient cards, sidebar, and reading surfaces; the brightest layer.
Primary action#285EA8Review buttons, brand mark, active navigation text, and keyboard focus.
Action hover#204B86A deeper shade for button hover in a future implementation.
Soft accent#E7EFFBSelected navigation, overview panel, and patient initials.
Main text#20324CHeadings, patient names, and essential reading content.
Secondary text#56677FTimestamps, descriptions, identifiers, and explanatory labels.
Dividers#DAE3F0Quiet card outlines and separators; not the only cue for controls.

Patient priority stays consistent across every option

High #B4233C on #FDECEF — a restrained raspberry red. Medium #8A5700 on #FFF4D6 — deep amber on pale cream. Low #246B48 on #EAF5EE — forest green on a very light green tint.

Reserve these colors for clinical status. Always pair color with written priority labels so meaning does not depend on color perception.

What makes the composition feel calmer

Keep roughly 85–90% of the interface in white and near-white surfaces. Use the brand color sparingly for navigation and the next action. Rounded 12–14px cards, generous spacing, subtle borders, and minimal shadows make the patient queue easy to scan. The light sidebar reduces the visual weight of the existing dark navigation. These are proposed styling choices shown only in this preview.

Use a familiar sans-serif with 16px patient names, 34px page titles, and clearly subordinate metadata. Keep clinical warnings small but explicit. A welcoming experience should come from clear hierarchy and useful next steps; color alone cannot guarantee engagement.

Preview scope

This is a standalone static design study. The option links and palette jump link work; dashboard controls are visual samples. No application routes, styles, or patient data have been changed. The preview includes proposed spacing and surface refinements so you can judge each palette in context.
